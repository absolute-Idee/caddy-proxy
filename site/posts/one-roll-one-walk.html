One roll, one walk

July 4, 2026 · 4 min read

This spring I gave myself a rule: one roll of film per walk, and the walk isn't over until the roll is. Thirty-six frames, no more, no fewer.

It sounds like a constraint, and it is, but it changed how I look. Early in the walk I'm stingy and frames are precious. By the end I'm photographing fences, puddles and a bus stop because I have eleven frames left and two kilometres to go. Those late frames are often the ones I like best.

What I took with me

One body, one 50mm lens, one roll of HP5 rated at box speed. A small notebook for exposure notes, which I abandoned by frame nine.

What I learned

Walking slowly is a skill. So is stopping. Most of the photographs I'm happy with came from standing still for a minute longer than felt comfortable.

I've done eleven of these walks so far. I'll post a few of the frames once I've finished scanning them, which, given the previous post, may take a while.
