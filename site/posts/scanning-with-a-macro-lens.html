Scanning negatives with a macro lens, two years in

September 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Two years ago I sold my flatbed and switched to "camera scanning": a digital camera, a macro lens, a light source and something to hold the film flat. I wrote down what I'd change if I started again.

The light matters more than the lens

I spent a long time comparing lenses and almost no time thinking about the light. A cheap LED panel with a poor colour rendering index made colour negatives far harder to invert than any lens difference ever did. A proper high-CRI light fixed more problems than anything else I bought.

Flatness, then focus

If the film isn't flat, no amount of careful focusing saves the corners. A simple holder with anti-newton glass on one side was the second-best purchase. I focus at the centre using a magnified live view and stop down to f/5.6 or f/8.

Dust

Dust always wins. A blower, an anti-static brush and a clean room help, but I've stopped chasing perfection. A few minutes with the healing brush is faster than twenty minutes of cleaning.

The script

The repetitive part is renaming and sorting files by roll. A tiny script does that for me:

for f in DSC*.ARW; do
  n=$(printf "%02d" "$i")
  mv "$f" "roll-${ROLL}-${n}.arw"
  i=$((i+1))
done

Nothing clever, but it saves me from doing it by hand every weekend.

Good enough and finished beats perfect and still on the copy stand.
